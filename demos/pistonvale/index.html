Something feels wrong

Repair is for problems you can already hear, see, or feel. Describe them early; small symptoms are easier to trace than big ones.

Good time to call
A new noise, a vibration, a fluid spot under the car, or a change in braking or handling.
Have ready
Year, make, and model, and when the problem shows up: cold starts, highway speed, turning, or braking.
Ask about
What was found, what the options are, and what can wait versus what shouldn't.

Staying ahead of it

Maintenance is the scheduled care your owner's manual recommends. It's the least dramatic work a shop does, and often the most valuable.

Good time to call
A maintenance reminder on the dash, an upcoming road trip, or a service interval you're unsure about.
Have ready
Your current mileage and, if you have it, when the last service was done.
Ask about
What your vehicle's schedule calls for at this point and what to plan for next.
Next step
Call (573) 555-0100 to talk through what's due.

Finding the cause

Diagnostics is careful investigation: confirming what's actually wrong before parts get replaced, especially for warning lights and problems that come and go.

Good time to call
A check-engine or other warning light, or a problem that appears only sometimes.
Have ready
Which light came on, whether it's steady or flashing, and what the car was doing at the time.
Ask about
What the findings point to and what you'd recommend doing first.
Next step
Call (573) 555-0100 and describe the light or symptom.

Before you call

A few details make the first conversation faster.

  • Your vehicleYear, make, model, and approximate mileage.
  • What you're noticingThe sound, smell, light, or feel, in your own words.
  • When it happensStarting up, at speed, turning, braking, or all the time.
  • How long it's been going onSudden changes are worth mentioning first.

If a warning light is flashing or the vehicle doesn't feel safe to drive, mention that right away when you call.
