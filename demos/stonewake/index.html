Raw ground
made ready.

Before a foundation, driveway, or utility line, the ground has to be shaped. That's where a project begins.

Know the ground

Soil, slope, access, and drainage all affect how a site should be prepared. A conversation about the property comes before any equipment moves.

Know what's below

Underground utilities need to be located before digging. In Missouri, that starts with a request to Missouri 811.

Capabilities in detail

Choose a category to see what it generally involves and what's useful to have ready when you call.

Excavation

Removing soil so a project has the space, depth, and shape it needs. The right approach depends on what's being built and the ground it sits in.

Useful to describe
What the excavation is for, its rough size, and how equipment would reach the area.
Worth asking
What happens with excavated material and how the site is left afterward.

Grading

Shaping the surface to planned levels and slopes. Good grading considers how water will drain across and away from the site.

Useful to describe
The area involved, any drainage problems you've noticed, and whether there are plans or elevations to follow.
Worth asking
How the finished grade will handle runoff.

Site Preparation

Turning raw ground into a workable base for construction, which can include clearing, shaping, and establishing a level working area.

Useful to describe
What's on the site now, what will be built, and any timing that depends on other trades.
Worth asking
What the site should look like when it's ready for the next step.

Utility Preparation

Groundwork that makes room for utility lines and connections, planned around existing underground lines located through Missouri 811.

Useful to describe
What kind of line is planned, where it needs to run, and the surface it crosses.
Worth asking
How the work will be coordinated with locates and other contractors.
